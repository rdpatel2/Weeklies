# M0: Does Weeklies save time and help people stay within budget?

Weeklies should make planning meals less work and help people avoid spending
more than they intended. Those are the two benefits this milestone focuses on.
A calendar full of suggestions is only useful if checking the suggestions,
fixing them, and adding up the prices does not take away those benefits.

For this first check, we used ten fictional planning sessions. The numbers below
come from a deterministic simulation, not from people using the app. The purpose
was to build and exercise an evaluation that could reject our hypothesis, then
make it easy to repeat with real observations.

## What we decided to measure

The task is to plan seven dinners. The baseline is choosing meals manually from
the same restaurant menus and recording them in a blank calendar. The Weeklies
condition uses meal generation and the calendar. Each person has the same
budget in both conditions.

Our claim is that Weeklies makes this task at least **30% faster**, measured by
the median of each person's time reduction. It must also produce complete plans
within budget for at least **80% of participants**, improving on the manual
baseline by at least **20 percentage points**. At least 80% must finish a suitable
plan in each condition, and we need at least five paired sessions.

All of those checks must pass. Finishing quickly does not make an over-budget
plan a success. Leaving meals out does not count as saving money, either.

These thresholds are project targets, not findings from previous research. A
30% time reduction should be large enough to be useful; the budget targets ask
for both a high success rate and an improvement over manual planning. Five
pairs are enough for an initial check, not a reliable estimate for every user.
The current policy is `m0-time-budget-v2`, which adds budgeting to the earlier
time-only draft without lowering the time target.

## Exactly how the sample was made

The generator is [scripts/simulate_m0.py](../scripts/simulate_m0.py). It creates
profiles numbered `i = 0` through `9`, displayed as `sim-01` through `sim-10`.
There is no random draw or external data source.

1. **Assign manual planning time.** Each profile gets 35 seconds of setup, then
   seven rounds of browsing and recording a meal. Browsing takes `60 + 5*i`
   seconds per meal, and recording takes another 25 seconds. The total is
   `35 + 7 * (60 + 5*i + 25)` seconds.
2. **Assign time with Weeklies.** Each profile gets 90 seconds of setup,
   `60 + 20*i` seconds of generation wait, and 45 seconds to review each of seven
   meals. Corrections add `(i % 4) * 60` seconds, where `%` means remainder.
   The total is `90 + (60 + 20*i) + 7*45 + (i % 4)*60` seconds. These are assumed
   durations, not measurements of the model or interface.
3. **Assign a budget and prices.** The budget is `10000 + 500*(i % 3)` cents,
   cycling through $100, $105, and $110. Manual plans cost $5 below budget for
   profiles 0–4 and $10 above budget for profiles 5–9. Weeklies plans cost $5
   above budget for profiles 2, 6, and 9, and $8 below budget for everyone else.
   These totals are invented; they were not calculated from the restaurant database.
4. **Include an incomplete plan.** All manual plans contain seven suitable meals.
   The Weeklies plan for profile 7 (`sim-08`) contains only five; the others
   contain seven. This checks that a cheap partial plan cannot improve the result
   unfairly. Meal suitability is assigned in this simulation, not inspected by
   the application.
5. **Record the paired sessions.** The generator writes
   [synthetic.csv](../evals/m0/synthetic.csv), labels every row `synthetic`, and
   attaches a reference to the generating profile. The task-order field alternates
   between manual first and Weeklies first. That field prepares the format for a
   pilot; the simulation does not model a practice effect.

These are the resulting inputs. Times are in seconds and costs are in dollars.
Every manual plan contains seven suitable meals.

## How the evaluator scored it

[scripts/eval_m0.py](../scripts/eval_m0.py) reads the CSV and checks the input
before calculating anything. It rejects missing fields, duplicate participants,
invalid times or costs, and files that mix synthetic and pilot observations.
Times must be positive and no more than 1,200 seconds; meal counts must be 0–7.
Budgets are positive integer cents. Costs are nonnegative integer cents, and a
complete plan must have a positive cost.

An incomplete plan receives a time score of **1,200 seconds**, even if the
session ended earlier. That makes `sim-08`'s Weeklies score 1,200 rather than
785 seconds. The participant stays in the sample.

For each pair, time reduction is:

```text
time reduction = 1 - (Weeklies scored seconds / manual scored seconds)
```

For example, `sim-01` saves `1 - 465/630`, or 26.19%. The evaluator takes the
median of all ten reductions. It does not divide the two groups' median times.

A budget success requires seven suitable meals and a total cost at or below the
assigned budget. `sim-08` fails that check even though $97 is below its $105 cap,
because two meals are missing. Both budget rates use all ten sessions. Their
difference is measured in percentage points, not percent savings in meal prices.

The report records the policy, thresholds, input SHA-256, individual scores,
summary numbers, and each check's result. The saved output is
[result.json](../evals/m0/result.json).

## What happened

| Check | Result | Target | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Median time reduction | 20.20% | At least 30% | Fail |
| Weeklies complete plans within budget | 6/10, or 60% | At least 80% | Fail |
| Improvement over manual budget success | 10 points: 60% versus 50% | At least 20 points | Fail |
| Suitable-plan completion | Manual 100%; Weeklies 90% | At least 80% each | Pass |
| Paired sessions | 10 | At least 5 | Pass |

The overall result is **FAIL**. The synthetic assumptions do not meet the targets.
That demonstrates the evaluation's failure path. It does not show that actual
Weeklies users save 20.20% of their time, or that 60% stay within budget.

## How to reproduce the numbers

Run these commands from the repository root with Python 3.11 or newer. The
instrument uses only the standard library; it needs no database, model, API key,
or installed application dependencies.

```bash
python -m unittest discover -s scripts/tests -v
python scripts/eval_m0.py evals/m0/synthetic.csv --output /tmp/m0-result.json
```

The evaluation command should exit with **1**, because this sample fails the
hypothesis. Exit 0 means every check passed. Exit 2 means the input was invalid
or a file could not be read or written.

To regenerate the data and compare both files:

```bash
python scripts/simulate_m0.py > /tmp/m0-synthetic.csv
diff -u evals/m0/synthetic.csv /tmp/m0-synthetic.csv
diff -u evals/m0/result.json /tmp/m0-result.json
```

Both comparisons should produce no output. All ten evaluator tests passed
locally. They cover passing and failing outcomes, threshold boundaries,
incomplete plans, malformed evidence, exit codes, and reproduction of the saved
sample and report.

## What we would do next

The decision for this synthetic review is **re-plan the next experiment**. Keep
the time-saving and budgeting goals, and keep the thresholds. The next useful
step is to replace the assumed waiting times, correction costs, and meal totals
with observations from at least five intended users.

Each person should perform both planning tasks with the same menu snapshot,
preferences, and budget. Set the budget before either task and check that the
catalog offers a feasible seven-meal plan at that price. Alternate task order and
use comparable date ranges to reduce practice effects. Start timing when the
task is presented, include generation waits and all corrections, and stop at
completion or 20 minutes. Keep abandoned and incomplete sessions in the sample.

An observer should check the seven date slots against the participant's stated
preferences and the menu's allergens, availability, and restaurant hours. Record
itemized prices, taxes, and mandatory fees using the same fulfillment method in
both tasks, without placing paid orders. Save those observations with anonymous
session IDs and evidence references. The evaluator consumes the recorded totals
and meal counts; it does not independently verify them.

Use the existing CSV columns, label the new rows `pilot`, and store them in a
separate file. Run the same evaluator without changing the thresholds. This
would let us see whether waiting, correcting suggestions, or choosing affordable
meals is the main obstacle before deciding what to change in the product.
